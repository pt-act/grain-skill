# Critique

Step 8. The meter is not the eye. Open every screenshot the inspector saved and write this critique before fixing anything.

## §1 The fourteen passes

Go down the list in order. Each pass gets a verdict: good, fix, or cut.

1. **The first screen.** Does it answer the visitor's first question in large type? Is the big thing big?
2. **The hero.** Exactly as the blueprint describes, or drifted? Does the picture earn its size?
3. **The idea.** Can you name the idea in one sentence by looking at the page? If not, it is not there yet.
4. **The live thing.** Is it findable on a phone with a thumb? Does it prove its claim?
5. **The headlines.** Two lines or fewer at this width? Do they say something, or describe furniture?
6. **The fullness.** Each screen: one dominant thing and the content it carries. Any screen mostly empty ground?
7. **The sections.** Do neighbours repeat each other's layout? Does the idea reach pricing, FAQ and footer?
8. **The pictures.** Real and honest, or placeholders and stock? Are they large enough to be places?
9. **The colour.** Ground, ink, accent doing their jobs? Accent under 5% of the area?
10. **The type.** Three sizes, nothing in between? Left edges shared? Running text in the full ink?
11. **The phone.** Its own composition, or a squeeze? Everything reachable, nothing overflowing?
12. **The close.** Does the footer return the name and answer who, where, when?
13. **The motion.** Present and explained in one sentence each — or decoration?
14. **The doubt.** The one thing you are least sure about. Write it down. It is usually right.

## §2 The score

Score the page out of ten, honestly, then say what the score is held back by:

```
Score: 7/10.
Held back by: the idea is only on the first screen; the FAQ and footer are stock.
```

A page below 5 needs a section rebuilt, not a detail fixed. A page at 7 with one clear problem is one fix away from 8.

## §3 The caps

Three problems, fixed in order, one round at most three. Fix the problem that hurts the page's reason for existing first — the idea, then the first screen, then the rest. Do not fix the FAQ before the hero is right.

## §4 The law of the picture

Judge only the pictures. If a problem is visible in a screenshot, it is real, whatever the code says. If a screenshot shows a section that is mostly empty ground, the section is underbuilt — do not argue with the frame.

## §5 Writing it down

Keep the critique in the project (`.grain/critique.md`). Write it in the format above, fix the top three, re-inspect, look again. When the page passes the gate and scores 8 or above with no doubt left, it is done. Below 8, say what still falls short in the report — an honest 7 beats a claimed 9.
