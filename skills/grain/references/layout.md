# Layout

The grid, the margins, and how sections differ from one another.

## §1 The grid

One grid for the whole page. On desktop:

- Margins: 24–32px at the edges of every section, always the same.
- Content: a max width of 1120–1280px for text; images and heroes run wider or to the edges.
- Two or three left edges shared by text blocks across the whole page. A block that starts at a fourth position is a mistake or a deliberate plate.

Phones: margins of 16–20px, one column, the same sizes as desktop scaled down (display 40–56px, text 15–17px).

## §2 The vertical rhythm

- The gap between sections is clearly larger than any gap inside one: 120–220px on desktop, 80–140px on a phone.
- Inside a section, one rhythm: 16, 24, 40. No 17, no 28. Rhythm is felt, not seen.
- Large things run to the edges: a hero, a photograph, a field of flat colour. Text blocks hold the margins. The contrast between the two is what makes the page feel built.

## §3 Section variety

Neighbouring sections must not share a layout. The dealt blueprints in the sheet are different on purpose: a ledger of entries, then a hang of objects, then a margin note, then a sequence. If you find two adjacent sections that look alike, one of them is wrong — change the composition, not the styling.

A page is four to eight sections. Below four, the idea does not have room; above eight, the page is a list and the idea is gone.

## §4 The hero

Build the hero exactly as its blueprint says, at desktop and phone width, before anything else. The hero is the page's thesis: if the hero is right, the sections are easier; if the hero is a generic full-bleed image with centred type, no section will save it.

The big thing is big. If the headline is not the largest thing on the first screen by a clear margin, it is not a headline.

## §5 Navigation

A plain bar: wordmark, three to five plain links, one action. No bar chrome — no floating pill, no backdrop blur, no shadow. The bar sits on the ground, separated by space or a tone step.

## §6 The footer

A designed close, not a sitemap. The common shape: the subject's name returns at full width, large; beneath it the columns (contact, hours, credits, legal) set small; the pictures' credits in the smallest type on the page. The footer answers: who, where, when, and what to do next.

## §7 The phone composition

The phone layout is its own composition, not a squeezed desktop. Decide per section: which element leads (the picture first, or the heading first), what moves to the top of the stack, what drops. A margin column moves above its content or drops. A triptych becomes a stack, left panel first. Test at 390px and measure the overflow — the inspector fails a page wider than its phone.

## §8 Rest

One deliberate rest per page at most: one screen that is mostly ground, earned by the sections around it. Two or more near-empty screens read as underbuilt. A rest is not a section with nothing in it; it is a breath, held for a reason.
